# Bread from zero

## Part 2: The bake

Part 1 made a dough and made it rise. This part turns it into bread.

---

## Lesson 3: Shaping

**After this lesson you can:** shape a proofed dough into a loaf that holds itself up.

**New terms:**
- **Bench rest:** twenty minutes a dough sits on the counter between a rough shape and the final one, so it relaxes enough to be shaped again.
- **Surface tension:** the tight skin you pull across the top of a loaf, which is what lets it stand instead of spreading.
- **Dough** (from Lesson 1): flour and water mixed until no dry flour is left.

Tip the proofed dough onto the counter and fold it into a rough ball. Give it a bench rest. Then flip it, fold the far edge to the middle, and roll it toward you, pulling the top tight as you go. That tightness is surface tension, and a loaf without it spreads in the oven.

**Try this:** shape two loaves, one pulled tight and one left loose, and bake them side by side after Lesson 4.

---

## Lesson 4: The oven

**After this lesson you can:** bake a loaf and read what its inside tells you.

**New terms:**
- **Oven spring:** the last rise a loaf takes in the first ten minutes of heat, before the crust sets.
- **Crumb:** the inside of a baked loaf: the holes, their size, and how they are spread.

Heat the oven as hot as it goes, with a heavy pot inside. Put the loaf in the pot, cover it, and bake for twenty minutes, then uncover it and bake until it is dark. The covered minutes are the oven spring: steam keeps the crust soft while the loaf grows.

Let it cool for an hour before you cut it. Then read the crumb. Big uneven holes mean a well proofed, high hydration dough. A tight, even crumb with a dense band at the bottom means the proof was too short.

**Try this:** cut the two loaves from Lesson 3 and compare their crumb.

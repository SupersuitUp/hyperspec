Voice memo transcript, recorded by the author on a walk, lightly cleaned. Raw thinking.

My first one-on-one as a manager was a disaster, and the reason was simple: I ran it. I had a
list. I went down the list. Project status, blockers, the thing from Tuesday. Thirty minutes
later my report said "cool, thanks" and left, and I had learned nothing I could not have read in
the tracker.

What I wish someone had told me: the first one-on-one is the only meeting where they get to set
the agenda. If you set it, you have told them what the meeting is for, and it is for you.

The three questions I use now. What is taking more of your energy than it should? What do you
want to be doing more of in six months? What should I stop doing, or start doing, that would
make your week easier? Then I shut up.

Status goes in the tracker. If a one-on-one is a status meeting, cancel it and read the tracker.

Aside, probably not for this piece: my second manager used to walk the one-on-ones outside. I
liked it but I do not think it is the point.

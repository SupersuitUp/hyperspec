Course brief, written by the author after teaching two friends to sail.

Nobody learns a knot from a picture. They learn it from the names of the parts of the rope, because every instruction for a knot is said in those names: the working end goes over the standing part.

Two knots carry a beginner a long way: one that joins two ends, and one that makes a loop that will not slip. Teach the join first, because it is the simpler of the two.

Each lesson ends with the knot tied three times without looking, because a knot you have to look at is a knot you will tie wrong in the dark.

I once taught the bowline before anyone knew which end was the working end, and the whole afternoon went on that one word.

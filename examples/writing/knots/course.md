# Two knots

## This course on one page

Two knots carry most of what a beginner needs on a boat. The first joins two ends of a rope so the join lies flat and comes undone with one pull. The second makes a loop at the end of a rope that keeps its size when you pull on it, and still unties afterwards. Each lesson names the parts of the rope before it asks you to tie anything, and ends with one knot to tie three times without looking.

---

## Lesson 1: The reef knot

**After this lesson you can:** join two ends of a rope and check that the join is right.

**New terms:**
- **Working end:** the end of the rope you move while you tie.
- **Standing part:** the rest of the rope, which stays where it is.
- **Reef knot:** two working ends crossed right over left, then left over right, so the knot lies flat.

Hold one end in each hand. Cross the right working end over the left and tuck it under. Then cross the left end over the right and tuck it under again. Pull both ends away from each other.

If the knot twists and will not lie flat, you crossed the same way twice. That is a granny knot, and it slips. Untie it and cross the other way on the second pass.

**Try this:** tie a reef knot three times with your eyes closed, and open them each time to check it lies flat.

## Lesson 2: The bowline

**After this lesson you can:** make a loop at the end of a rope that holds when you pull on it and still unties.

**New terms:**
- **Bight:** a bend in the rope that does not cross itself.
- **Bowline:** a fixed loop at the end of a rope, made by passing the working end up through a small loop, around the standing part and back down through the small loop.

Make a small loop in the standing part, with the working end lying on top. Pass the working end up through the small loop, around behind the standing part, and back down through the small loop. Hold the working end against the side of the big loop and pull the standing part.

Pull hard on the big loop and look at the knot. The working end should sit inside the big loop. If it sits outside, the knot can roll over when you pull on it, so start again.

**Try this:** tie a bowline around a chair leg, pull hard on the standing part, then push the bight behind the knot to loosen it and untie it.
